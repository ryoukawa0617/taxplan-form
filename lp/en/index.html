WHY IT IS REFUNDED

Why you get the money back

1. You were taxed as a non-resident

By the time the payment was made, you had already left Japan. So a flat 20.42% was withheld with no deductions at all.

2. The law lets you recalculate it

Japan's Income Tax Act lets non-residents choose to be taxed on this payment as retirement income instead. This is called elective taxation on retirement income (退職所得の選択課税).

3. The deduction usually makes the tax zero

This method first subtracts a retirement income deduction (¥400,000 per year covered, at least ¥800,000). For most people the deduction is larger than the payment, so the tax becomes zero and the full 20.42% comes back.

The refund is your right by law. But it is not automatic. A tax return has to be filed in Japan, and because you live overseas, a tax agent in Japan (納税管理人) must be appointed first. That is what we do for you.

FAQ

Questions

Do I really pay nothing in advance?
Yes. Our fee comes out of the refund. If the tax office issues no refund, we do not charge you anything.
How long does it take?
Usually 2–3 months from the day your original notice reaches our office. International post often takes time, so please send it as soon as you can.
Do I need to be in Japan? Do I need a Japanese bank account?
No to both. We act as your tax agent in Japan, so the return can be filed and the refund received while you live overseas. We then send the money to your own account.
Will I get the full 20.42% back?
In most cases the retirement income deduction is larger than the payment, so the full amount withheld is refunded. High earners may pay some tax, but still get over 90% back. However, if you also received a retirement allowance from your employer in the same year, it is calculated together with the payment and the refund can be much smaller. Tick the box in the estimator to check.
I lost my Notice of Lump-sum Withdrawal Payments. Can I still apply?
Yes, you can still apply. We will explain how to get it re-issued after you apply. It helps if you have your Basic Pension Number or pension book.
How long do I have? I left Japan years ago.
You can claim for 5 years from January 1 of the year after you received the payment (Income Tax Act, Article 173; Act on General Rules for National Taxes, Article 74). If you received it in — or later, you can still apply.

This is often confused with the deadline for the Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment itself (2 years after leaving Japan), but they are different deadlines.
Who am I dealing with? Are you really a licensed tax accountant?
EAST TAX Accounting Office, a licensed tax accountant office in Tokyo. Anyone can confirm our registration by entering No. — on the official search site of the Japan Federation of Certified Public Tax Accountants' Associations. Our address and contact details are at the bottom of this page.
Where in the law is this refund based?
Mainly Income Tax Act Article 171 (elective taxation on retirement income) and Article 173 (refunds under elective taxation). A detailed explanation with the legal text is available in Japanese on our 解説ページ.
