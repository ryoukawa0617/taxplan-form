Why you get the money back

1. You were taxed as a non-resident

By the time the payment was made you had left Japan, so a flat 20.42% was withheld with no deductions applied at all.

2. The law lets you re-calculate it

Japan's Income Tax Act lets non-residents elect to be taxed on this payment as retirement income (退職所得の選択課税) instead.

3. The deduction usually wipes out the tax

That method first subtracts a retirement income deduction — ¥400,000 for every year you were covered, minimum ¥800,000. For most people it is larger than the payment itself, so the tax becomes zero and the whole 20.42% comes back.

The refund is yours by law — but it is not automatic. Someone has to file a Japanese tax return for you, and because you are overseas, a tax agent in Japan (納税管理人) has to be appointed first. That is exactly what we handle.

Questions

Do I really pay nothing in advance?
Correct. We are paid out of the refund itself. If the tax office issues no refund, you pay nothing — you will not receive an invoice.
Could I just do this myself?
Yes. You are free to file the return yourself, and it costs nothing to do so. What you would need to handle is the Japanese-language return, appointing a tax agent in Japan, corresponding with the tax office, and receiving the refund into a Japanese bank account. Our fee is for taking all of that off your hands.
How long does it take?
Usually 2–3 months from the day your original notice reaches our office. International post is often the slowest part, so send it as soon as you can.
Do I need to be in Japan? Do I need a Japanese bank account?
No to both. We act as your tax agent (納税管理人) in Japan, which is what allows the return to be filed and the refund to be issued while you are overseas. We then remit the money to your own account.
Am I guaranteed to get the full 20.42% back?
In most cases the retirement income deduction is larger than the payment, so the entire amount withheld is refunded. It can be less if, for example, you also received a retirement allowance from your employer in the same year, or your covered period was long with high remuneration. We check your actual figures before filing and tell you plainly what to expect.
I lost my Notice of Lump-sum Withdrawal Payments.
That is a common situation. Our partner labor and social security attorney can request a re-issue from the Japan Pension Service for ¥2,000, also deducted from your refund. You will need your Basic Pension Number or pension book if you still have it.
How long do I have to claim?
Up to five years from the year the Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment was made. After that the right to claim the refund is lost.
Who exactly am I dealing with?
EAST TAX Accounting Office, a licensed tax accountant (税理士) practice in Tokyo. Registration details and our office address are in the footer of this page.
